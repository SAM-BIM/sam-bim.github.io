Open-source computational engineering

From BIM geometry to auditable engineering workflows

SAM is an open-source computational-engineering platform built around a shared analytical building model. It connects BIM, building physics, HVAC systems and simulation through reusable, traceable workflows.

What makes it different

  • One model, many workflowsGeometry, constructions, systems and results live in one analytical model that every workflow reads and extends.
  • Explicit engineering dataSpaces, adjacency, systems and their relationships are named, inspectable objects — not hidden tool state.
  • Deterministic and auditableWorkflows are repeatable and record what they used, so results can be reviewed and reproduced.
  • BIM and simulation connectedAuthoring tools and simulation engines are adapters around the model, not separate islands of data.
  • Decisions keep their authorityRegulatory requirements, engineering design and equipment capability stay distinct. See the principle
  • Open, scriptable, extensibleLGPL-licensed .NET code, scriptable from Grasshopper and C#, extended through independent modules.

What SAM does

Six capability families, each built on the same analytical model and implemented in public repositories.

Analytical modelling

Spaces, panels, apertures, adjacency, materials, constructions, profiles, internal conditions, systems and results in one model — inspected and edited in the SAM desktop apps.

  • SAM_UI

BIM & geometry interoperability

Analytical models from Revit 2025–2027 and Rhino 8/9, with IFC, gbXML, IES VE GEM, BHoM and Ladybug Tools exchange. A watertight Open Cascade geometry kernel is in development.

  • SAM_Rhino_UI
  • SAM_IFC
  • SAM_gbXML

  • SAM_OCCT

Building performance

Loads, ventilation, overheating and thermal-simulation workflows on the model, including Part F ventilation traced to Approved Document F and Part O overheating assessed with CIBSE TM59.

  • SAM
  • SAM_SolarCalculator

HVAC & systems

Building systems as explicit engineering networks: plant rooms, air handling and ventilation, catalogue equipment selection, and psychrometric air processes on the Mollier chart.

  • SAM_Systems
  • SAM_Mollier
  • SAM_Psychrometrics

Simulation interoperability

Tas integration for zone and systems simulation, with results read back onto the analytical model. Native OpenStudio conversion is in development.

  • SAM_OpenStudio

Automation & computational engineering

Repeatable, scriptable workflows: a large Grasshopper component library, .NET APIs, scenarios run in series or in parallel, and data exchange for large models.

Flagship workflow

One example of SAM in practice: Part F to Part O

A three-dwelling model taken from ventilation requirement to overheating result. The analytical model, engineering logic, equipment data and simulation work together; each image is a real SAM screen.

  1. Analytical model
  2. Regulatory requirement
  3. Engineering design
  4. Equipment decision
  5. Simulation
  6. Engineering result
  1. 01 Regulatory requirement

    Part F calculates what must be achieved.

    SAM derives the Approved Document F airflow for each room from the model and shows it on the plan beside the design.

    Authority: Regulation
  2. 02 Engineering design

    The engineer sets the design intent.

    Calculation proposes; the engineer reviews. Here design airflow is raised above the requirement to address overheating, while the requirement and the unit’s capacity stay unchanged.

    Authority: Engineer
  3. 03 Equipment decision

    SAM selects equipment and prepares the scenario.

    Each dwelling gets the smallest catalogue unit that meets its design duty; the unit’s maximum is a ceiling, never a design value. SAM then builds a repeatable simulation scenario and records its state.

    Authority: Product
  4. 04 Simulation & result

    Tas simulates; SAM assesses the result.

    The building is simulated in Tas and each room assessed against CIBSE TM59. On 2050s design weather this case still fails in 6 of 8 rooms — and SAM says so.

    Authority: Simulation

On the 2026-Q3 release line, the same design can be simulated through inter-zone airflows or through explicit ventilation systems in Tas, including selected-product cooling, with the unchanged TM59 assessment. Technical record

SAM supports engineering workflows that contribute to assessment methods; compliance depends on the complete project methodology. A TM59 pass is an overheating result, not a Part O compliance certificate.

Engineering principle

Different engineering concepts stay different objects

A requirement is not a design, and a design is not what equipment can deliver. SAM keeps each quantity as its own object with its own authority, so one value never silently becomes another. The flagship workflow shows it with airflow:

01

Required

The minimum the regulation demands.

Authority: Regulation
02

Design

What the engineer decides to ventilate at.

Authority: Engineer
03

Selected capacity

What the selected unit can deliver — a ceiling, not a target.

Authority: Product
04

Operating

What flows in the simulated or commissioned system.

Authority: Operation
