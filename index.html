Open source · Computational engineering · Building performance

From BIM geometry to auditable building-performance workflows

SAM (Sustainable Analytical Model) is an open-source analytical-model and computational-engineering platform. It connects BIM, building physics, HVAC engineering and simulation through deterministic, tested and traceable workflows — so every number in a result can be followed back to the decision that produced it.

Flagship workflow

Part F to Part O / TM59 in one analytical workflow

One analytical model carries a dwelling from the regulatory airflow requirement, through the engineering design decision and equipment selection, to dynamic simulation and a CIBSE TM59 result — with each step's authority kept separate and inspectable.

Four quantities, four authorities

Four airflow figures share a unit and mean different things. SAM holds them as four separate, auditable quantities — a catalogue capacity never rewrites a design decision, and an operating value never masquerades as a requirement.

Required airflow

What the regulation demands as a minimum.

Design airflow

What the engineer decides the dwelling will be ventilated at.

Selected capacity

What the chosen equipment can deliver — a ceiling, not a target.

Operating airflow

What actually flows in the simulated or commissioned system.

Platform

One analytical model, multiple engineering workflows

SAM is a .NET platform, not a plugin. Authoring tools and exchange formats feed one shared analytical model; engineering logic and simulation adapters hang off it. Grasshopper is one host among several — not the foundation.

Engineering depth

Beyond model conversion

In development

Native OpenStudio conversion

Direct SAM-to-OpenStudio model generation: geometry, constructions, schedules and thermostats with ideal-air loads. HVAC airside and plant are not yet covered; Tas remains the primary simulation pathway. SAM_OpenStudio

In development

Native watertight geometry (SAM_OCCT)

An Open Cascade geometry kernel inside SAM: sewing and healing of open shells, booleans, STEP/IGES exchange — tested, with CI, not yet wired into the SAM core model. SAM_OCCT

In development

SAM supports engineering workflows that contribute to assessment methods. Compliance still depends on the complete project methodology and toolchain. A TM59 pass is an overheating assessment result, not a Part O compliance certificate.
